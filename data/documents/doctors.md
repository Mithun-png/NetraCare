# NetraCare doctors and specialities

NetraCare has 5 doctors. This document says which speciality looks after which kind of eye problem, and which doctors cover each speciality.
A doctor always assesses the patient. The front desk and the assistant only suggest which speciality to book.

## Choosing a speciality

- Describe what you notice in your own words. The front desk suggests a speciality, and the doctor assesses you at the visit.
- If you are not sure, book General. A General doctor can see any eye problem and refer you on inside the clinic.
- Children under 16 see the Paediatric doctor, whatever the problem.
- If you have had a visit before, book a follow-up with the same doctor.
- Some symptoms need urgent attention instead of an appointment. See "Emergencies" in the clinic policy and FAQ.

## General

Who it is for:
- Routine eye tests and new glasses or a change of power
- Mild redness, watering or itching
- Headaches when reading, or eyes that tire easily
- Anyone who is not sure which speciality to book

Doctors: Dr. Anand Rao, Dr. Kiran Shah, Dr. Vikram Menon.

## Cataract

Who it is for:
- Vision that has become cloudy, misty or dull slowly, over months or years
- Glare or dazzle from headlights when driving at night
- Colours looking faded or yellowish
- Needing brighter light to read, or glasses that keep changing
- Patients who have been told they may need cataract surgery, or who want a check after it

Doctors: Dr. Anand Rao, Dr. Meera Iyer.

## Glaucoma

Who it is for:
- An optician or doctor said the eye pressure is high
- A parent, brother or sister has glaucoma
- Patients already using glaucoma eye drops, for regular reviews
- Long-term use of steroid tablets or drops, for a pressure check

Doctor: Dr. Meera Iyer.

## Retina

Who it is for:
- People with diabetes, for a yearly diabetic eye check or a review
- Straight lines looking wavy or bent, slowly getting worse
- A blurred or dark patch in the centre of the vision that has come on gradually
- Patients over 60 who find reading or recognising faces harder over time

Doctor: Dr. Kiran Shah.

## Cornea

Who it is for:
- Problems with the clear front window of the eye
- A white spot or haze on the front of the eye that has been there for weeks or months
- Discomfort with contact lenses, or wanting to change the type of lens
- A cornea problem that runs in the family, or vision that keeps getting more distorted with new glasses

Doctor: Dr. Fatima Khan.

## Refractive (LASIK)

Who it is for:
- Adults over 18 who want to stop wearing glasses or contact lenses
- Checking whether laser vision correction (LASIK) suits you

Doctor: Dr. Fatima Khan.

## Dry eye

Who it is for:
- Gritty, burning or stinging eyes, often worse after screen work or air conditioning
- Eyes that water a lot, then feel dry
- Contact lenses that feel dry by the end of the day

Doctor: Dr. Fatima Khan.

## Paediatric

Who it is for:
- Any child under 16
- A child who sits too close to the TV, squints to see the board, or rubs their eyes a lot
- School eye checks and a child's first eye test

Doctor: Dr. Vikram Menon.

## Squint

Who it is for:
- An eye that turns in, out, up or down, in a child or an adult
- Double vision that has been there for a while
- A child's eyes not looking in the same direction in photos

Doctor: Dr. Vikram Menon.
