# NetraCare procedures: what to expect

This guide explains the tests and procedures done at NetraCare: what happens, how long you are at the clinic, what to do before and after, and the indicative cost.
Your doctor decides whether you need any of these. Costs are indicative, in Indian rupees, and the final amount is confirmed by Staff at the clinic. The assistant cannot take payments or handle insurance.

## Comprehensive eye examination

- What happens: a vision and glasses power check, eye pressure check, and a look at the front and back of the eye. Most patients have dilating drops (see "Dilation" in the clinic policy and FAQ).
- Who does it: any NetraCare doctor, at a normal consultation.
- Time at the clinic: about 1 to 1½ hours, including 30 minutes for the drops to work.
- Before: bring your glasses, contact lens case and any old reports. Do not wear contact lenses on the day.
- After: vision is blurred and bright light feels uncomfortable for 4 to 6 hours. Do not drive yourself home.
- Indicative cost: included in the consultation fee.

## Children's eye check

- What happens: vision tested with pictures or letters to suit the child's age, a squint check, and a glasses power check with drops that relax focusing.
- Who does it: Dr. Vikram Menon.
- Time at the clinic: about 1½ hours, because the drops take 40 minutes to work.
- Before: a parent or guardian must come. Bring the child's glasses and any school eye-check report. A snack and a favourite toy help.
- After: the child's near vision is blurred and the pupils stay large for up to a day. Sunglasses or a cap help outdoors.
- Indicative cost: ₹900, including the consultation.

## Diabetic eye check

- What happens: a dilated examination of the retina and an OCT scan, a painless photo-like scan of the back of the eye that takes about 5 minutes.
- Who does it: Dr. Kiran Shah.
- Time at the clinic: about 1½ hours.
- Before: eat and take your diabetes medicines as usual. Bring your latest sugar report (HbA1c) if you have one.
- After: blurred vision for 4 to 6 hours after the drops. Do not drive yourself home.
- Indicative cost: ₹1,800, including the OCT scan.

## Glaucoma tests (visual field and OCT)

- What happens: a visual field test, where you press a button each time you see a small light, and an OCT scan of the optic nerve. Eye pressure is checked too.
- Who does it: Dr. Meera Iyer.
- Time at the clinic: about 1 hour.
- Before: keep using any eye drops as usual, unless your doctor told you otherwise. Bring your drops with you. Get a good night's sleep, because the field test needs concentration.
- After: no special care. You can drive unless you were also given dilating drops.
- Indicative cost: ₹2,500 for both tests.

## Dry eye assessment

- What happens: questions about your symptoms, a test of how much tear you make (a small paper strip rests on the lower lid for 5 minutes), and a look at the tear film with a yellow dye.
- Who does it: Dr. Fatima Khan.
- Time at the clinic: about 45 minutes.
- Before: do not use any eye drops for 2 hours before the visit. Do not wear contact lenses on the day.
- After: no special care. The yellow dye may tint tears for an hour.
- Indicative cost: ₹1,500, including the consultation.

## Cataract surgery

- What happens: the cloudy natural lens is removed through a tiny cut and replaced with a clear artificial lens (an IOL). It is done under numbing drops or a small injection, and you are awake. Surgery itself takes about 20 minutes.
- Who does it: Dr. Anand Rao or Dr. Meera Iyer, on their surgery days.
- Time at the clinic: day care, about 3 to 4 hours. No overnight stay.
- Before: a pre-surgery check about a week before, with lens measurements and blood sugar and blood pressure checks. Continue your usual medicines unless the doctor says otherwise. Have a light breakfast. Come with an adult who can take you home.
- After, do: use the prescribed drops exactly as instructed, wear the eye shield at night for 1 week, wear dark glasses outdoors, and come for the check the next day.
- After, don't: rub or press the eye, let soap or water into the eye for 1 week, swim for 4 weeks, lift heavy weights or bend low for 2 weeks, or drive until the doctor says your vision is safe for it.
- Indicative cost: ₹35,000 to ₹90,000 per eye, depending on the lens chosen.

## LASIK (laser vision correction)

- What happens: first, a suitability check (about 1½ hours) with scans of the cornea. If suitable, a laser reshapes the cornea to correct the glasses power. Both eyes are usually done on the same day, under numbing drops. The laser part takes a few minutes per eye.
- Who does it: Dr. Fatima Khan. Only for adults over 18 whose glasses power has been stable for at least a year.
- Time at the clinic: day care, about 2 to 3 hours on the day of the procedure. No overnight stay.
- Before: stop soft contact lenses 1 week before the suitability check, and rigid (RGP) lenses 3 weeks before. No eye make-up, creams or perfume on the day. Come with an adult who can take you home.
- After, do: rest your eyes for the rest of the day, use the prescribed drops as instructed, wear the eye shields while sleeping for 1 week, and come for the check the next day.
- After, don't: rub your eyes, wear eye make-up for 1 week, swim for 2 weeks, or drive until the doctor says your vision is safe for it.
- Indicative cost: ₹45,000 to ₹1,10,000 for both eyes, depending on the laser method. The suitability check is ₹2,000.

## Squint surgery

- What happens: the muscles that move the eye are adjusted so the eyes line up better. Children have general anaesthesia (fully asleep). Adults usually have local anaesthesia.
- Who does it: Dr. Vikram Menon.
- Time at the clinic: day care, about 5 to 6 hours, including time to wake up from anaesthesia. No overnight stay in most cases.
- Before: no food for 6 hours and no water for 2 hours before surgery. A parent or guardian must stay with a child for the whole day. A check with the anaesthetist is done a few days before.
- After, do: use the prescribed drops, keep the eye clean, and come for the check after 1 week.
- After, don't: swim for 2 weeks, rub the eye, or let dusty or dirty water into it. Redness for 2 to 3 weeks is expected.
- Indicative cost: ₹40,000 to ₹70,000, depending on how many muscles are adjusted.

## Retina injection

- What happens: a medicine is placed inside the eye with a very fine needle, after numbing drops and cleaning. The injection takes a few seconds. Many patients need a series of injections, about a month apart.
- Who does it: Dr. Kiran Shah.
- Time at the clinic: day care, about 2 hours.
- Before: eat and take your usual medicines. Do not wear eye make-up. Come with an adult who can take you home.
- After, do: use any prescribed drops and keep the follow-up appointment.
- After, don't: rub the eye or swim for 3 days. Do not drive on the day of the injection.
- Indicative cost: ₹8,000 to ₹30,000 per injection, depending on the medicine.
