What you'll build

Your iteration 1 website, plus an LLM for three jobs: a Fact of the Day for patients, a safety check on every booking, and a summary for doctors.

Patient

  • See a Fact of the Day about eye health on login, with the caveat "General information, not medical advice"
  • Click Refresh to get a new fact
  • Give a reason when booking an appointment
  • See the emergency response if the reason is a red flag

Staff

  • Enter the reason when booking for a patient
  • See urgent cases at the top of the Staff view, and mark them seen
  • See an Urgent tag on red-flag appointments

Doctor

  • Write a note when marking a visit completed
  • Open a patient from their schedule and see an AI summary of the profile, today's reason and past notes from all doctors
  • See the full past notes below the summary

R4

Red-flag check with the LLM

  • What a red flag is: a symptom that may mean the patient could lose sight if they wait for a normal appointment. They must be told to come now. NetraCare uses the 5 categories in the brief (section 07):
CategoryRed flag when the patient describesNot a red flag
sudden_vision_lossVision dropping or going dark suddenly (within minutes, hours or a day), in one or both eyes. "I suddenly can't see from my left eye", "everything went dark"Blurring that came on slowly over months or years
curtain_or_shadowA curtain, veil or shadow coming over or moving across part of the vision. "Like a blind pulled down over half my sight"A small fixed spot the patient has had for years
floaters_or_flashesA sudden shower of new floaters (dots, spots, cobwebs), or flashes or sparks of light. "Lots of new floaters since yesterday"A few old floaters that haven't changed
chemical_or_injuryAny chemical in the eye (cleaner, bleach, acid, cement) or any injury: hit, poked, or something flew in. "Floor cleaner went in my eye"Mild irritation from dust or smoke that has settled
severe_pain_headache_haloesSevere eye pain, especially with headache, feeling sick or vomiting, or haloes and rainbows around lights. "My eye is throbbing and I threw up"Mild itching, grittiness, dryness or tired eyes
  • The patient must describe it as happening now. "No sudden loss of vision" is not a red flag.
  • When the model is unsure, it should flag. A false alarm costs Staff one click; a missed red flag is never acceptable.
  • Write one LangChain chain, check_red_flags(text), in its own file: a prompt template, the chat model at temperature 0, and an output parser.
  • The prompt explains what a red flag is and describes the 5 categories above, so the model can decide whether the reason describes one of them in any wording.
  • The output parser returns data, not free text: red_flag (true or false), category (one of the 5 examples, or none) and a one-line why. A Pydantic model with PydanticOutputParser, or with_structured_output, both work.
  1. A patient or Staff clicks Book. Before any clinic rule is checked, the backend runs check_red_flags on the reason.
  2. On a red flag, the screen shows the emergency response first, in red, word for word:

What you describe needs urgent attention. Please come to the clinic now or go to the nearest emergency department. Our staff have been notified.

  1. The backend saves an urgent case: patient, reason text, category, the model's why and the time. Urgent cases sit at the top of the Staff view in red until Staff click "Mark seen".
  2. The booking then goes through the normal rules check. If it is booked, it carries an Urgent tag for Staff and the doctor. If it is refused, the urgent case still stands.
  • If the check fails (timeout, API error, or a reply the parser can't read), never book silently. Refuse the booking with "We couldn't check your reason just now. Please try again, or call the clinic if it's urgent."
  • Iteration 3's chat and iteration 4's agent call this same function, so keep it on its own.

Check: the red-flag rows in section 09 behave as described. reason_samples.csv has 35 more reasons to try if you want to test further.

R5

Fact of the Day

  • When a patient logs in, show a "Fact of the Day" card with one general fact about eye health, in no more than two sentences, from the LLM.
  • A Refresh button on the card calls the LLM again and shows a new fact. Send the current fact in the prompt and ask for a different one, so the same fact doesn't come back.
  • The prompt asks for general, friendly facts only: no conditions to look out for, no treatments, medicines or product names.
  • Your code adds the caveat "General information, not medical advice" under every fact. Never ask the LLM to write it.

Check: each patient login shows a fact with the caveat under it, Refresh shows a different fact, and no fact gives advice to treat anything.

R6

Doctor summary

  • When a doctor opens a patient from their schedule, show a short summary of no more than 120 words, written by the LLM.
  • Into the prompt: the profile (age from year of birth, general health, allergies), today's appointment reason, and every completed visit from all doctors (date, doctor, speciality, note), newest first.
  • Prompt rules: only restate what is recorded. It may repeat a diagnosis or treatment a doctor recorded, and should say which doctor. It never adds its own diagnosis, guess, risk or advice. If something isn't recorded, it leaves it out.
  • Label it "AI summary of recorded notes. Check the notes below." and show the full past notes underneath.
  • Only a doctor can get a summary. The backend checks the role.

Check: the summary rows in section 09 behave as described.

05

Rules and safety in this iteration

This iteration delivers the brief's red-flag rule (section 07) and starts two more targets from section 09: 100% of red-flag messages trigger the emergency response, and 0 answers that name a condition or suggest treatment. Iteration 1's target still holds: 0 bookings that break a clinic rule.

Safety rules

  • Red flags always get the emergency response before any booking, and appear as urgent on the Staff view.
  • Nothing a patient sees names a condition or suggests treatment. That covers the Fact of the Day now, and the chat in later iterations.
  • The doctor summary is for doctors only. It may repeat what a doctor recorded, but it never adds its own diagnosis or advice.
  • The LLM never decides a booking. It only flags a reason as urgent. Every booking still goes through the iteration 1 rules check.
  • If the red-flag check can't run, no booking is made.
  • Patients only ever see their own appointments and details. A patient cannot get a summary or another patient's notes, even by calling the API directly.
  • Use only the data packs. Never send real patient information to an LLM API.
  • Keep your API key in a .env file listed in .gitignore. Commit a .env.example with the key left blank.

07

Technology choices

PartUse
LLM frameworkLangChain, basics only (required): a prompt template, a chat model and an output parser, joined into one chain with |. Use a string parser for the Fact of the Day and the summary, and a Pydantic parser for the red-flag check. No agents, memory or RAG yet.
LLM providerAny you have access to, such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini or Groq, or a local model with Ollama. Read the provider and model name from .env.
Everything elseAs in iteration 1.
TestingBy hand, using the checks in section 09. Automated tests are a stretch goal.

Put all LLM code in one module, for example llm.py, with one chain each for the Fact of the Day, the red-flag check and the summary. Give each call a timeout. If the fact or summary call fails, the app keeps working: the card shows a fixed fallback fact, and the summary says "Summary unavailable. See the notes below." If the red-flag call fails, no booking is made (R4).

Suggested new endpoints (adapt as you like): GET /fact-of-the-day (with an optional previous fact for Refresh), GET /doctor/patients/{id}/summary, POST /appointments/{id}/complete (with the note), GET /urgent-cases, POST /urgent-cases/{id}/seen.
